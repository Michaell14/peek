Blinkfn or esc to close FlowFreeform Edit
  1. You are halfway through a sentence.
  2. Checking one thing means switching apps, then finding your place again.
  3. Hold fn. The things you check appear over what you are doing.
  4. Let go. You are still in your sentence, and now you know the number.
  5. Websites stay signed in and refresh on a timer, so they are current when you look.
  6. Clocks for the cities you work with. Reminders to tick off. Today on your calendar.
  7. PDF and HTML files too. Save the file and the tile shows the new version.
  8. Tap fn instead and it stays open, so you can click, scroll and type.
  9. Flow keeps your tiles in tidy columns. Freeform lets you put them anywhere.
  10. It opens over full-screen apps too, and leaves them as they were.

    Private, free and open source.

    Private

    No account, no analytics, no server. Your layouts and everything you browse in Blink stay on your Mac.

    Free

    There is no paid version and nothing to unlock. Download it and it is yours.

    Open source

    The code is on GitHub under the MIT licence. Read it, change it, or build it yourself.

    Also in Blink

    Quieter pages
    Third-party ads and trackers are blocked and cookie banners hidden. Click any part of a page to hide it for good.
    The right display
    It opens on the display with your pointer, the one with the active window, or one you pick.
    Your own shortcut
    Keep fn, choose any key combination, or use one modifier on its own, such as the right ⌘.
    Open it elsewhere
    Send any tile to your browser, or a file to the app that usually opens it.

    Questions

    How do I install it?

    Download the disk image, open it and drag Blink into Applications. Blink is not notarized by Apple yet, so macOS stops it the first time you open it. Go to System Settings, then Privacy & Security, and choose Open Anyway. You only do this once.

    Why does it ask for Input Monitoring?

    macOS only shows the fn key to apps you allow under Input Monitoring. Blink watches for its shortcut and does not record what you type. If you would rather not allow it, pick an ordinary combination such as ⌥ ` in Settings, which needs no permission. With fn as the shortcut, set System Settings, Keyboard, “Press globe key to” to Do Nothing.

    How does it update?

    Once a day Blink asks GitHub for the newest release and offers to install it. The request carries nothing about you or your Mac. Check for Updates in the menu bar asks straight away, and Settings, About turns the daily check off.

    What does it know about me?

    Nothing it sends anywhere. There is no account, no analytics and no server. Websites you add are stored like a browser stores them, on your Mac, and so are your layouts.

    Is it really free?

    Yes. Blink is open source under the MIT licence, with no paid tier. You can also build it from the source with Xcode 16.

    Which Macs does it run on?

    Any Mac with macOS 14 Sonoma or later, Apple silicon or Intel.

02 Two gestures

Hold to look.
Tap to stay.

Hold

Blink is there for exactly as long as the key is down. Nothing to close, nothing to switch back from.

Tap

A quick tap keeps it open so you can scroll, click and type. Tap again, or press esc, when you are done.

04 Arrange

Arrange it once.

Flow packs tiles into tidy columns for you. Freeform lets you put anything anywhere. Switch between them and nothing jumps.

05 Less noise

See the number, not the page around it.

A glance should not have to get past a cookie banner. Flip the switches, or click anything in the page to make it go away.

06 Yours

Free, open, and it stays on your Mac.

  • No account. Nothing to sign up for, nothing to sign in to.
  • No analytics. Blink has no server. Your layouts and browsing data never leave your Mac.
  • No price. MIT licensed. Use it, change it, ship your own.
  • No surprises. It asks for a permission only when a feature needs it, and says why.

07 Get Blink

Two commands and it is in your menu bar.

Needs macOS 14 or later and Xcode's command line tools.

git clone https://github.com/Michaell14/peek.git blink
cd blink && make install

Prefer not to build it? Every change is built automatically: download the latest build (needs a GitHub account).

08 Questions

Fair things to ask.

Does it work over full-screen apps?

Yes. Blink appears over whatever is in front, full-screen spaces included, and does not pull you out of the app or the text field you were in.

How is this different from keeping tabs open?

Tabs are somewhere you go. Blink comes to you, shows several things side by side, and is gone the moment you let go. The pages stay loaded in the background, so there is nothing to wait for.

What permissions does it need?

None to start. The Calendar and Reminders widgets ask for access when you add them. A shortcut made of a single modifier key needs Input Monitoring, because macOS only tells apart left and right keys that way; ordinary shortcuts need nothing. Blink looks for your shortcut and does not record what you type.

Which sites work?

Blink uses WebKit, the engine behind Safari, so nearly everything that works there works here. Sign-in flows that depend on a separate pop-up window are the main exception.

What is not there yet?

Passkeys in web tiles, browser extensions, a stocks widget, and showing other apps' windows as tiles. Builds are not notarized yet either, so macOS asks you to confirm the first launch.

Is it really free?

Yes. The whole thing is on GitHub under the MIT license. There is no paid tier and nothing to unlock.

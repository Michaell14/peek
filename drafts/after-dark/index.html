The calendar can wait ten seconds. So can the inbox. What cannot wait is the sentence you are halfway through, which is gone the moment you leave it to check

Everything you check, one key away.

Blink is a small Mac app for the pages, lists and calendar you look at all day. Hold fn and they appear over whatever you are doing. Let go and you are back where you were.

Download for Mac Free and open source
macOS 14 or later

Hold the key. Or hold Space on your keyboard. Press and hold the key. Let go to close.

You go to them.

Mail, the calendar, a dashboard, a list, a PDF. Every look is a trip to the Dock, then a hunt for where you were.

Now they come to you.

Blink keeps them loaded in one layer over everything. Hold a key and they are all there at once.

Hold to look. Let go and carry on. Tap to keep it open.

Hold fn and Blink opens over whatever is in front, full-screen apps too.

The app you were in never lost focus. Your cursor is where you left it.

Tap fn instead and Blink stays, so you can click, type and scroll. Tap again or press esc to close.

What it holds.

Websites, files and a few small widgets. They stay loaded while hidden, so there is nothing to wait for.

  1. Websites Any site, still signed in. This one refreshes itself every 30 seconds.

  2. Clock The time where the people you work with are.

  3. Calendar Today as a timeline, with a line for now.

  4. Reminders Tick things off without opening anything. Go on.

  5. Notes A scratchpad one key away. Type something.

  6. Files PDF and HTML files, reloaded when they change on disk.

Put things where your eyes go.

Flow packs tiles into tidy columns for you. Freeform lets you drag and resize them anywhere, and they snap into line. Switching keeps everything where it is.

Questions.

How do I install it?

Download the disk image, open it and drag Blink into Applications. Blink is not notarized by Apple yet, so the first time you open it macOS will stop you. Go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and choose Open Anyway. You only do this once.

Why does fn need a permission?

macOS only shows the fn key to apps you allow under Input Monitoring, so Blink asks when it first opens. It watches for the shortcut and nothing else, and does not record what you type. Choose an ordinary combination such as ⌥` instead and no permission is needed. If fn also opens the emoji picker, set the globe key to Do Nothing in System Settings → Keyboard.

How do updates arrive?

Once a day Blink asks GitHub whether there is a newer release and offers to install it and restart. The request carries nothing about you or your Mac. Check for Updates in the menu bar asks straight away, and Settings turns the daily check off. After an update macOS may ask for Input Monitoring again.

What does Blink know about me?

Nothing it sends anywhere. There is no account, no analytics and no server. Your layouts, sign-ins and browsing data stay on your Mac.

Is it really free?

Yes. Blink is open source under the MIT license, with no paid tier and nothing to unlock. You can build it yourself from the source.

What do I need?

A Mac running macOS 14 or later. The Calendar and Reminders widgets ask for access when you add them, and not before.

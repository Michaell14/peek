    The rest, in short

    Blink opens on the screen you are using, answers to whatever shortcut you choose, keeps pages free of ads and cookie banners, and hands any tile to your browser. It works over full-screen apps. It keeps everything on your Mac, and it is free.

    Displays
    Opens on the display with the pointer, the one with the active window, or one you pick. One layout everywhere, or one for each display.
    Any shortcut
    fn by default. Any key combination, or modifier keys on their own, down to a specific left or right one: Right ⌘ Left ⌥ ⌃ ⌥ B
    Less noise
    Blocks third-party ads and trackers and hides cookie banners. Click anything on a page to hide it for good.
    Hand off
    Any tile opens in your browser, or a file in its usual app, from the tile's ⋯ menu.
    Over full-screen apps
    Blink appears over whatever is in front, full-screen spaces included, and you stay in the app and the text field you were in.
    Private
    No account, no analytics, no server. Your layouts and browsing data stay on your Mac.
    Free and open source
    MIT licence. Read the code, or build it yourself with one command.

    Questions

    How do I install it?

    Download the disk image, open it, and drag Blink into Applications. Blink is not notarized by Apple yet, so the first time you open it macOS will stop you. Open System Settings, go to Privacy & Security, and choose Open Anyway. You only do that once.

    Why does it ask for Input Monitoring?

    For the fn key. macOS only shows fn, and shortcuts made of modifier keys alone, to apps you have allowed under Input Monitoring. Blink looks for the shortcut and does not record what you type. Choose an ordinary combination in Settings and it needs no permission at all. Calendar and Reminders ask for access when you add those tiles.

    How do updates arrive?

    Once a day Blink asks GitHub whether there is a newer version, and offers to install it and start again. The request carries nothing about you or your Mac, and you can turn it off in Settings. After an update macOS may ask for Input Monitoring again.

    What does Blink know about me?

    Nothing leaves your Mac. There is no account, no analytics and no server. Your layout, and the cookies and logins of the sites in your tiles, are stored on your Mac.

    Is it really free?

    Yes. The whole app is on GitHub under the MIT licence. There is no paid version, and you can build it yourself from the source.

    Is this page the app?

    No. The screen above is drawn in HTML so you can try the ideas. The websites on it are stand-ins and nothing you do here is saved. The app itself uses the real sites, your own calendar and reminders, and real files.
